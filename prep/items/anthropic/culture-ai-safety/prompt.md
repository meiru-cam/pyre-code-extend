This is a 55-minute conversation with one interviewer, one of the rounds in the onsite loop. Nothing is submitted beforehand. What you bring is a specific, considered answer to why you want to work at Anthropic and on AI safety, a decision process that holds up as a hypothetical dilemma is pushed further with follow-up questions, a defensible opinion — including real disagreement — on the company's own published safety commitments, a set of personal stories with concrete stakes, and a separate read on your character and judgment, including one prompt argued live in the room. The questions fall into five groups.

### Motivation and mission

- Why Anthropic, specifically — as opposed to another lab or company working on related problems?
- Which one of Anthropic's stated values resonates with you personally, and why — with a concrete example of when you acted on it, not merely admired it?
- Why does AI safety matter to you personally, rather than as a cause that would fit any AI employer?
- How does your past work — the specific projects, decisions, or field you came from — actually connect to that concern?
- What would make you want to leave Anthropic?
- What could Anthropic do better?

### Hypothetical dilemmas

- Which risk from more capable AI do you personally take most seriously, and what evidence would change your view of it?
- Your team produces a genuine capability breakthrough, but its risk is not yet well understood or quantified. Would you delay releasing it for safety reasons, and on what basis?
- Do you support slowing down AI progress broadly for safety, even where the decision is not solely yours to make?
- What do you do if you hold back and a competing lab does not?
- What do you do if that choice leaves you, or your team, visibly behind?
- Separately: you are caught between a commercial deadline and a safety, reliability, or privacy concern you believe is real. How do you decide what to do?
- If Anthropic's equity value fell to zero, or the company faced a severe operating or financial crisis, how would you respond — in your own work, and in how you communicate with your manager and team?

### Critical engagement with Anthropic's public commitments

- Anthropic publishes its own safety commitments, including a scaling policy that sets out when it will restrict or pause deploying a model that has become more capable. Which specific part of a commitment like that do you agree with, and why?
- Which specific part would you change — tighten, loosen, or argue against entirely — and what would that change trade away?
- Have you engaged with Anthropic's own public reasoning about AI safety and its approach to training a model's values? What do you actually think of it?

### Values stories

- Tell me about a time someone persuaded you to change a position you had genuinely held and argued for — what changed your mind?
- Tell me about a working relationship that broke down, and how you repaired it.
- Tell me about a time you raised a safety, reliability, or privacy concern that slowed down or blocked a launch.
- Tell me about a time you disagreed with a decision made by someone with authority over you, and pushed back on it.
- Tell me about a mistake you made where you were the one who was wrong, and how you handled it.
- Tell me about a moral dilemma you actually faced — two obligations pulling in different directions — and how you decided between them.
- Tell me about something you did that you now judge to have been wrong, though it did not feel that way then, and how you have reconciled it since.
- Tell me about a time you held to a value under real, uncomfortable pressure to abandon it.

### Character and judgment

- What has been the most personally transformative experience of your life, inside work or outside it, and what changed in how you operate afterward?
- Tell me about something you keep doing purely because you care about it, with no practical payoff.
- Name the task you enjoy least — then, once the interviewer says they dislike it too, persuade them to do it anyway.
- What is the most complex situation, technical or otherwise, that you have handled?
